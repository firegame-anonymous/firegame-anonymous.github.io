FireGameLearning Implicit State Transitions
for Game World Models

An action-conditioned game world model that learns how game states evolve from gameplay video and uses them to generate mechanics-consistent gameplay.

Watch comparisons Review evaluation

How game states evolve

In fighting games, the same input can have different effects depending on the current state. A special attack may fail, and a round must end when a fighter’s health reaches zero. FireGame learns these state changes from gameplay video and uses them to guide generation.

Learn transition codes

The Game Transition Tokenizer (GTT) learns discrete codes by reconstructing changes between consecutive frames. A frozen GTT provides targets for transition prediction.

Predict states under actions

The model initializes its latent state from the first frame and rolls it forward under player actions. State-Guided Cross-Attention (SGCA) lets the video diffusion model attend to that state sequence.

Refine mechanics and visuals

Stage 1 updates the latent transition model and SGCA using the mechanics reward, with the video backbone frozen. Stage 2 freezes those modules and refines the video backbone using both rewards.

Reward model and rollout filtering

The reward model learns from 24,000 generated clips across 6,000 conditioning groups. It assigns separate scores for mechanics fidelity, such as skill legality and health changes, and for visual quality, such as temporal coherence. Stage 2 weights these scores by 0.6 and 0.4, respectively.

Both stages retain a group only if its best mechanics score is at least 50 out of 100 and the gap between its best and worst mechanics scores is at least one point. The filtering ablation below measures the effect of removing this step.

Evaluation protocol

StreetBench uses Street Fighter III: New Generation, and FightBench uses The King of Fighters ’98. Each mechanics test set has 100 five-second clips at 20 FPS. The test sets are held out from world model and reward model training.

Macro Fail is game-specific. In StreetBench, a super input is issued with insufficient meter and the move fails to start. In FightBench, the super executes but misses.

Mechanics fidelity
Gemini-3.8-flash and GPT-6.1-sol classify each clip into one of the five scenarios. We report classification accuracy over three runs for each judge.
Recorded-State Visual Agreement (RVSA)
A frozen game-specific HUD reader extracts both fighters’ health and energy, plus the timer. The score compares their trajectories with the recorded game states.
Visual quality and action control
SSIM and LPIPS compare generated frames with the reference. Move-Acc and Att-Acc use separate single-action test sets of 100 clips each per game.

Benchmark results

FireGame achieves 89.0% and 89.3% mechanics fidelity on StreetBench, and 99.7% under both judges on FightBench. Fine-tuned ReactiveGWM has the highest movement accuracy on both benchmarks.

Ablation studies

Adding the transition tokenizer improves both judges’ mechanics scores on both benchmarks. Progressive reinforcement learning raises them further. On StreetBench, removing mechanics-aware filtering reduces SSIM from 0.604 to 0.544.
